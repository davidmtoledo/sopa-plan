Strategic Plan 2025–2035  |  Pillar 1

Enrollment Growth

Grow SoPA to 500 majors by 2035 through curricular revision, focused recruitment, and retention efforts.

Pillar 1 Goals & Objectives
Full Goal Breakdown
Enrollment Growth establishes the strategic foundation for growing the Collinsworth School from 284 majors in 2025 to 500 by 2035 — the threshold at which SoPA can sustain a comprehensive faculty, justify facility investment, and fund the full range of programming that defines a premier performing arts school. Growth is pursued through program expansion, deliberate recruitment, and retention infrastructure designed to bring new student populations into SoPA while ensuring current students persist to graduation.

1.1 Achieve 500-Major Enrollment by Fall 2035  View Forecast →

SoPA's ten-year enrollment trajectory targets 500 total majors by FA35 — achievable through a combination of organic growth, program restructuring, and new program development. Reaching 429 by FA30 serves as the mid-decade checkpoint for assessing pace and making course corrections.

  • Grow total enrollment to 350 or above by FA28, representing early-stage validation that growth strategies are taking hold across all divisions
  • Reach 429 total majors by FA30, reflecting steady division-level progress and the launch of new programs beginning to contribute enrollment
  • Achieve 500 total majors by FA35 — anchored by Commercial Music and Production reaching 156 majors, Music Education 92, Performance and Pedagogy 110, Worship Arts 82, and Theatre Arts 65
  • Monitor division-level pace annually and implement corrective actions when any division falls more than 15% below projection for two consecutive years
1.2 Expand Program Architecture to Reach New Student Populations

Organic growth within existing programs will not be sufficient to reach 500 majors. The most significant enrollment gains in the forecast come from deliberate program expansion: new degrees, restructured programs, and double-major pathways that open SoPA to students who would not previously have had a pathway into the school.

  • Restructure the existing BM Studio Production into the BS Production Design and Technology, eliminating the audition requirement and adding a Live Production concentration
  • Develop and launch the BS Entertainment Industry Studies as a joint program with the School of Business — targeting students passionate about the entertainment industry who do not identify as performers or producers
  • Integrate the BM Songwriting into BM Commercial Performance as an optional Songwriting Concentration by FA27; manage the BM Songwriting teach-out through FA27
  • Pursue WSCUC approval for BS Entertainment Industry Studies by October 2026, targeting a Fall 2027 launch
  • Launch the MM Music Education (online/hybrid) in FA26, targeting steady-state enrollment of 24 students by FA30
  • Launch the Doctor of Worship Studies (online/hybrid) in FA27, positioning SoPA as a national leader in graduate-level worship arts formation
  • Sustain MA Worship Arts enrollment at steady state (22–24 students) through intentional recruitment within denominational and church networks
1.3 Build Enrollment Infrastructure and Pipeline Systems

SoPA must build the recruitment relationships, audition infrastructure, scholarship tools, and retention systems that turn prospective students into enrolled majors and enrolled majors into completers.

  • Establish division-specific recruitment relationships with high schools, community colleges, community arts organizations, and church networks
  • Develop a scholarship strategy by division that positions SoPA competitively in its primary recruitment markets
  • Complete and maintain current four-year degree plans for all SoPA majors (target: published by FA26)
  • Develop and maintain competitive analysis documentation for each program, updated annually
  • Partner with CBU Admissions to develop SoPA-specific recruitment collateral, audition event experiences, and digital presence
1.4 Improve Student Retention Through Engagement Tracking and Targeted Intervention

Enrollment growth is undermined when students leave before completing their degrees. Reaching 500 majors requires not only recruiting students into SoPA programs but keeping them enrolled and progressing toward graduation.

  • Identify and track meaningful engagement metrics for first-year students including applied lesson attendance, ensemble participation, advising contact frequency, and academic standing
  • Establish a first-year student engagement review process conducted at the mid-point of each semester beginning SP27
  • Track six-year graduation rates by program and division, disaggregated where possible by entry year, scholarship status, and concentration
  • Target: first-year retention rate of 85% or higher across all divisions by Year 5
  • Target: improve six-year graduation rate from current baseline by 10 percentage points by FA35
Years 1–3: Foundation
  • Four-year degree plans completed and published
  • BS Entertainment Industry Studies submitted for approval; FA27 launch
  • MM Music Education launches FA26 with inaugural cohort
  • First-year engagement metrics established and data collection initiated
  • Division-specific recruitment plans documented
Years 4–7: Expansion
  • BS EIS reaches 40+ majors; Commercial Music surpasses 100
  • Doctor of Worship Studies at full first cohort
  • Total enrollment reaches 429 by FA30
  • First-year retention rate at 85%+ across all divisions
Years 8–10: Preeminence
  • 500 total SoPA majors by FA35
  • Commercial Music and Production reaches 156 majors
  • Six-year graduation rate improved 10+ percentage points from FA25 baseline
